# Batch 01 — Sequencing Concerns

The teacher's grammar-concept introduction order for §§2–20 is preserved in the lesson plan. No silent resequencing.

The two observations below are flagged here for transparency. Neither results in a sequencing override.

## 1. Transfer-pattern clustering (not a grammar concern)

The teacher places `-ity → -idad` at §12 and `-ary → -ario` at §13, interleaved with grammar rather than clustered up front with `-al`, `-ant/-ent`, `-mente`, `-ible/-able`, `-tion`. Since transfer patterns are explicitly packaging (AI's call, per the division of authority), I considered front-loading them.

Chose to **match the teacher's placement** for two reasons:
1. Lessons 13–14 still sit inside the "first third" of the batch, meeting the interleaving directive.
2. Front-loading all cognate patterns risks vocab-density overload before the learner has enough conjugation to use cognate nouns in sentences.

Not logged in `improvements.md` since nothing diverged.

## 2. Article/gender gap — resolved by gap-fill, not resequencing

The teacher uses `la casa` (§7/§8), `las casas` (§12), and `el coche` (§27 — outside batch 1) without explaining grammatical gender or the article system. The full `-a feminine` rule does not appear until §23 (outside batch 1).

This creates a gap in batch 1 where learners parse `a la casa` and `las casas grandes` without the rule. Two options considered:

- **A. Silently advance the `-a feminine` rule into batch 1.** Rejected — violates the sequencing constraint.
- **B. Add a minimal `article-el-la-basic` filler rule in the lesson where articles first appear (L8).** Chosen — the filler covers *just enough* for the learner to parse `la/las/el/los` as definite articles agreeing with the noun, without pre-empting the teacher's full `-a feminine` treatment in batch 2. See `batch-01-gaps.md`.

This is gap-filling, which the division of authority explicitly grants AI. The `-a feminine` rule itself is left for batch 2.

---

Everything else in batch 1 is pedagogically coherent in the teacher's order:

- Modals introduced as atomic chunks (`quiero` L4 → `debo` L9 → `puedo` L14 → `tengo que` L15) before the regular yo form is generalized at L12.
- Pronoun placement taught postverbal-only at L5 → flexibility revealed at L12.
- Stem changes taught after the regular skeleton (L17–18 after L12).
- Transfer patterns for cognate nouns arrive in the first half, allowing grammar lessons in the second half to draw on a wide vocab base.
