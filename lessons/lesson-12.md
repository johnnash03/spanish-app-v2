This is the conjugation skeleton the whole language hangs on. Keep it light: you only need a couple of shapes to start making real sentences.

## The "I" form: drop `-r`, add `-o`

- tolerar → tolero
- colaborar → colaboro
- exagerar → exagero

## The he/she/it form for `-ar`: drop the `-r`

- durar → dura
- eliminar → elimina
- masticar → mastica

## The "they" form: add `-n`

Take the he/she/it form and add `-n`.

- habla → hablan
- elimina → eliminan

That one move (`+ n`) is why you can read forms like `hablan` even before you've learned every person.

## Pronouns can move in two-verb sentences

When you have a conjugated verb + a to-verb, the object pronoun can go in either place:

- Quiero verlo / Lo quiero ver
- Debo invitarlo / Lo debo invitar

Both mean the same thing.

The key idea: **two verbs gives you two legal parking spots** for the pronoun.
