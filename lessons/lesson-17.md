Up to now you've memorized forms like `quiero`, `tiene`, `viene` without knowing the "why". This is the why: some verbs **split** their stem to add stress.

## e → ie: the stem splits to add stress

Think of it as: the verb wants to be louder in the stem, so it stretches the vowel.

- pensar → Pienso *(I think / I plan)*
- entender → Entiendo *(I understand)*
- perder → pierdo *(I lose)*

And you'll see it in the forms you already use:

- venir → vienes
- tener → tiene
- querer → quiero

You don't need a full chart yet. Just notice the split when you hear it and connect it back to the dictionary form.

One more place you see the same `ie` chunk is in regular words like:

- tiempo
- concierto
- noviembre

## `pasar` in the "what's going on?" sense

`Que pasa` is literally "what happens?" and it functions as "what's going on?"

- Que pasa
- Quiero saber que pasa

## `cuando` = when

- Cuando vienes — When are you coming?
- Cuando vienes a comer — When are you coming to eat?
