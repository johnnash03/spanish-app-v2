This is a big structural habit: some verbs "pull" an `a` before the next to-verb because they carry motion.

## `ver` = to see, and `los` = them

`los` is the plural of `lo`:

- Quiero verlos — I want to see them

## `voy a ...` = I'm going to ...

`voy` comes from `ir` (to go). With a following to-verb, it takes an `a` — even when you are not literally moving anywhere.

- Voy a verlos — I am going to see them

## Motion + `a` survives chains

If the first verb is a motion verb, you keep the `a` after it. The second verb might be a non-motion verb (like `intentar`) and it still does not take `a`.

- Voy a intentar verlos — I am going to try to see them

## Another motion verb: `pasar`

`pasar` (to pass by) also pulls `a` before the next to-verb.

- Voy a pasar a visitarte — I am going to pass by to visit you

## `mostrar` and `algo`

- Quiero mostrarte algo — I want to show you something
