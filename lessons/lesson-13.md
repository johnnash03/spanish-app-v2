This lesson is dense, but it's all practical: your first `-er` verb, a gender shortcut for `-ion` nouns, and the "me lo / nos lo" pattern.

## `vender` (to sell) is an `-er` verb

The he/she/it form ends in `-e`:

- vender → vende

And the "they" form is just `+ n`:

- vende → venden

## With one verb, the pronoun goes in front

When there is only one conjugated verb, the object pronoun cannot attach to anything — it sits before the verb.

- Los vendo — I am selling them
- Lo vende — He sells it

## Two pronouns: `me lo`, `nos lo`

When you have two object pronouns, the "to me / to us" piece comes first.

- No me lo venden — They are not selling it to me
- No nos lo venden — They are not selling it to us

## `-ion` nouns are usually feminine

If you see an `-ion` noun, default to `la`:

- la administracion
- la celebracion
- la asociacion

## -ity → -idad

Another transfer power-up:

- university → universidad
- possibility → posibilidad
