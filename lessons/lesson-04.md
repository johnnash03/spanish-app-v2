`quiero` is the workhorse for "I want to ...". Spanish keeps it simple: `quiero` + the verb in its dictionary form (the "to-verb").

## `quiero` + to-verb

- Quiero cancelar — I want to cancel
- Quiero preparar — I want to prepare

No extra word is needed between them.

## `querer` vs `quiero`

You'll see both:

- `querer` = the dictionary form (the to-verb)
- `quiero` = the form you actually say for "I want"

For now, treat `quiero` as a memorized chunk.

## Negation still goes right before the verb

Put `no` directly before `quiero`.

- No quiero cancelar — I don't want to cancel
- No quiero preparar — I don't want to prepare

## "Get ready" = "prepare myself"

When English says "get ready", Spanish often says "prepare myself". The `me` (myself) sticks to the end of the to-verb as one word.

- Quiero prepararme — I want to get ready
- No quiero prepararme — I don't want to get ready
