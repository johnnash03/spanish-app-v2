Two practical chunks land here: how to say "I know" in the present, and how to say "I have to ...".

## `se` = I know (from `saber`)

The regular pattern would have given you something like *sabo*. Spanish doesn't do that. The "I" form is:

- Lo se — I know it
- No se que quiere — I don't know what he wants

## `tengo que` = I have to ...

This one doesn't translate word-for-word smoothly. Just learn it as a chunk: `tengo que` + to-verb.

- Tengo que comer — I have to eat
- Tengo que hacerlo — I have to do it

And because there are two verbs, the pronoun can also park before the first one:

- Lo tengo que hacer — I have to do it

## Quick contrast: `debo` vs `tengo que`

Both can mean "I must / I have to", but the structure differs:

- Debo verla *(no extra word)*
- Tengo que verla *(the `que` is part of the chunk)*
