Four ideas land here: what Spanish verbs look like in the dictionary, a small "yo-go" family, a stress rule, and a simple way to talk about the future without "will".

## All Spanish to-verbs end in `-r`

In the dictionary, every Spanish verb ends in `-r`. You will see three families:

- `-ar` verbs
- `-er` verbs
- `-ir` verbs

Example: `comer` (to eat).

## The tener family

Once you know `tener` (to have), a bunch of longer verbs are basically "tener with a prefix":

- obtener
- contener
- sostener
- mantener
- entretener

## "Yo-go" verbs: `tengo`, `vengo`, ...

A small set of verbs has a special **I-form** that ends in `-go`.

- tener → Tengo
- venir → Vengo

Treat these as memorized chunks.

## Future without "will": use a time marker

Spanish can use the present tense plus a time marker like `mañana` (tomorrow) or `mas tarde` (later).

- Vengo mañana — I will come tomorrow
- Como con Pablo mañana — I eat with Pablo tomorrow

## Stress: the default rule

You don't need a lot of accent rules on day one. One default gets you far:

- words ending in a vowel, `n`, or `s` stress the second-to-last syllable
- words ending in other consonants stress the last syllable

That difference is why `intentar` and `intento` sound stressed in different places.
