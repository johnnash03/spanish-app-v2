You already have `quiero` (I want). Now you get the form that covers **he/she/it** and also **you (formal)**: `quiere`.

## `quiere` = he/she/it wants, and you (formal) want

Same surface form, different meanings depending on context.

- Quiere visitarme — You (formal) want to visit me

You still don't need a separate word for "he" or "she" when it's obvious who you're talking about.

## `venir` = to come

`venir` is a verb of motion. You'll see it both with destinations and with other to-verbs.

## "to the house" = `a la casa`

This is a noun phrase:

- `a` = to
- `la` = the
- `casa` = house

So:

- Quiere venir a la casa — Do you want to come to the house?

## `debo` = I must / I owe

Use `debo` directly with a to-verb. No extra word in between.

- Debo continuar — I must continue
- Debo identificarlo — I must identify it

If you can say `quiero` + to-verb, you can say `debo` + to-verb the same way.
