Now you can build "I want to know ..." sentences. And you get one tiny word that does two jobs.

## `saber` = to know

Use it after `quiero` the same way you use any other to-verb:

- Quiero saber — I want to know

And you can keep stacking:

- Quiero saber si quiere venir a la casa a visitarme — I want to know if she wants to come to the house to visit me

## `si` = yes, and `si` = if

Same spelling, two meanings. Context does the work.

- Quiero saber si quiere saber — I want to know if you want to know

As a standalone answer, `si` is simply "yes".

## One more object-pronoun reminder

When the object comes after a to-verb, it sticks to the end:

- No quiero imaginarlo — I don't want to imagine it

And you can do the same with your newer verbs:

- Quiero iniciarlo ahora — I want to start it now
